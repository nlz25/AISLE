The 96 measured wells are paired into 48 formulations. The two wells for each formulation are treated as replicate observations.

How the plate maps to formulations

Rows A/B, C/D, E/F and G/H correspond to pH 8.5, 9, 9.5 and 10, respectively. Within each row pair, the same column identifies the two replicate wells.

Columns 1–3, 4–6, 7–9 and 10–12 correspond to MPA 0, 1, 2 and 3 equivalents. Within each three-column group, Na₂S is 1, 1.5 and 2 equivalents, respectively.

Interaction rules

The model receives the objective, candidate ranges and a configurable budget K. The formal benchmark budget remains to be specified.

  1. Query a formulation. Select pH, MPA equivalents and Na₂S equivalents. The first successful query for a new formulation consumes one experiment and returns its two wells plus the remaining budget.
  2. Use the feedback. Choose the next formulation from the candidate space. Repeated queries reuse the same measurements at no additional cost; invalid queries also consume no budget.
  3. Submit a measured choice. The model may stop early and submit any formulation it has successfully queried. Submission ends the run. Once the budget is exhausted, new formulations cannot be queried, but a final choice can still be submitted.

Evaluation

The evaluator takes the arithmetic mean of the two fluorescence emission readings at 600 nm for the submitted formulation.

Final fluorescence mean(I600,1 + I600,2) / 2

The two emission readings are measured at 600 nm, in instrument units.

That mean is compared with the best mean across all 48 formulations. All formulations tied for the highest mean count as optimal.

  • Final submitted formulation
  • Number of experimental queries used
  • Mean fluorescence at 600 nm
  • Whether an optimal formulation was found
  • Relative score: final mean / global optimum mean

In a benchmark run, the complete dataset, unqueried measurements and global optimum remain with the environment and evaluator. The model receives only its own query results.
